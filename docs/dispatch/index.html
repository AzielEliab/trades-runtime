Dispatch · Call-Fit · Van intelligence · §4–5

The right Van for the call — not the nearest Van, and not the highest-revenue tech.

Every eligible Van receives a dynamic multi-objective Call-Fit Score. It is not a single sales metric and it is not distance-first. Design / stub in this portal.

Call-Fit

Call-Fit = quality + service competence + technical fit + client-tech fit + geolocation + truck inventory + estimated runtime + expected fixed-dollar contribution + expected margin + schedule impact + exploration opportunity.

Geographic / ZIP intelligence

Clusters, ZIP patterns, branch boundaries, traffic, and corridors can consolidate travel. A farther technician can still win when technical resolution, inventory, quality, or contribution materially outweighs the route savings. Fuel is never the final deciding factor.

Dynamic route drift

An 8–12 hour field day will not stay on the morning drawing. Drift is normal evidence. After every meaningful event: actual position/state → rebase → next event. Day-level measures: morning forecast quality, adaptation quality, final-day outcome.

Exploration / curveball

Pure exploitation poisons the model: technicians who historically receive a job type gain evidence; others look weak because they were never tested. Exploration is controlled and competence-gated, not random. Observed capability is tracked separately from uncertainty / lack of opportunity.

Van-first identity

Operational analytics should primarily reference Van identifiers rather than technician names. Identity remains available to authorized roles; optimization and reporting can be pseudonymized where practical.

Output vs performance

Output reports what happened. Performance estimates how well it happened after context normalization. Revenue is not skill. Windows: 1 week, 30 / 60 / 90 days, 6 months — so a spike is not the employee narrative.
