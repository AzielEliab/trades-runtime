                                                Trades-Runtime
                               Property, Neighborhood & Regional Intelligence Addendum v1.0

Scope: New Trades-Runtime property intelligence requirements added after the canonical tools/pipelines registry. This
document defines the living property record, valuation trajectory, neighborhood repair intelligence and regional repair-pattern
recalibration loop.


1. Property Intelligence Layer
Every client property becomes a modeled operational object rather than a static address. Trades may ingest authorized/public
property information from approved or licensed real-estate/property-data sources, county/assessor and permit records,
customer-provided documents, and the company's own service history and field evidence.

    Property data                Examples

    Structure                    Year built, square footage, lot size, property type, stories, basement/crawlspace/slab, garage and additions.

    Market history               Prior sales, listing history, market exposure, assessed value and comparable-property information.

    Documents & media            Listing/property photos, permits, disclosures or inspection material when legitimately available, and company before/during

    Trade context                HVAC, plumbing and electrical equipment/system age, known materials, prior diagnoses, repairs, warranties and company


2. Living Property Lifecycle Record
The property record updates as the customer relationship progresses. Repairs and replacements are not merely closed jobs;
they change the canonical technical state of the property.

  Property baseline -> current condition -> proposed work -> approved work -> work in progress -> completed
  work -> verified equipment/property state -> estimated property-value effect -> future maintenance/value
  trajectory

Each significant job can update removed/replaced assets, make/model/serial, capacity/efficiency, installation date, warranties,
permits, financing where applicable, before/after evidence, repair history, remaining useful life, operating-cost expectations and
subsequent risk.


3. Property Improvement Ledger
  Date -> Trade -> Work Performed -> Cost -> Asset Replaced/Modified -> Remaining Life Changed -> Warranty ->
  Permit -> Evidence -> Estimated Value Effect -> Confidence -> Later Realized Evidence

The ledger distinguishes Value Added, Value Preserved, Risk Removed, Operating Cost Reduction, Marketability
Improvement, and Safety/Code Improvement. Repair cost must never be assumed to equal resale-value increase.


4. Property Lifecycle & Value Intelligence Engine
Trades maintains a value range rather than a falsely precise number. Inputs can include external market estimates, assessed
value, recent comparable sales, last sale, internal condition adjustment, local market direction, property improvements and
source freshness.

  Pre-work property value -> repair/improvement cost -> avoided deterioration/risk -> estimated value
  preservation -> estimated value uplift -> marketability effect -> confidence range

When a property later lists or sells, Trades can settle prior estimates against realized market evidence and recalibrate its
improvement-value models.


5. Market & Neighborhood Value Model
    Layer                           Inputs

    Property-specific state         Age, sqft, lot, condition, systems, repairs, upgrades, warranties, permits, additions, deferred maintenance and company h

    Neighborhood state              Comparable sales, price/sqft trends, inventory, days on market, sale/list relationships, construction/permit activity and othe

    Local/regional market           ZIP/county/metro appreciation or decline, transaction volume, housing supply, rents where useful, construction and labor

    Macro state                     Inflation, mortgage rates, credit conditions, employment/income indicators and broader housing-market direction.


6. Property Value Trajectory
  Previous value estimate -> market movement -> neighborhood movement -> property-condition movement ->
  completed-work effect -> depreciation/aging -> macroeconomic adjustment -> current value range -> direction ->
  confidence

Trades should track whether value is rising or falling and explain the modeled contributors. Attribution remains estimated unless
supported by realized sale evidence. It should maintain both Nominal Home Value and Inflation-Adjusted / Real Value.

Mortgage rates should influence modeled buyer purchasing power and local demand pressure rather than being converted into a
simplistic fixed percentage deduction from property value.

7. Neighborhood Momentum & Property Condition
A Neighborhood Momentum Score can summarize recurring local market direction using comparable-sale trends, price/sqft,
transaction velocity, inventory, days on market, sale/list behavior, construction/permit activity and distress signals. Its
methodology and components remain visible.

A separate Property Condition Delta compares the subject property with appropriate nearby comparables while accounting for
verified or evidence-supported HVAC, plumbing, electrical and other material condition differences.


8. Neighborhood Failure Pattern Engine
Trades learns recurring repair/failure patterns among nearby homes with similar construction era, materials, utility infrastructure,
subdivision/builder history, soil/environment and property type.

    Trade                     Illustrative pattern classes

    Plumbing                  Clay sewer tile cracking/splitting, root intrusion, Orangeburg collapse, cast-iron deterioration, galvanized restrictions, polybutylen

    Electrical                Recurring panel/service/wiring issues, capacity limitations and construction-era electrical patterns.

    HVAC                      Recurring duct, sizing, insulation, condensate, equipment-location and equipment-family failure patterns.

The model separates Observed Neighborhood Pattern, Inferred Risk, and Verified Property Condition. A neighborhood
pattern is a diagnostic prior, not proof that the individual property has the defect.


9. Neighborhood Repair Risk Profile & Density Mapping
Each property can receive a Neighborhood Repair Risk Profile showing issue class, risk level, evidence basis, comparable case
count, geographic/era similarity and property-specific verification status. Repair-density maps can visualize patterns such as
sewer replacements, panel upgrades, water-service failures or other confirmed work by neighborhood/subdivision/build decade.


10. Regional Repair Pattern Recalibration Engine
Company findings continuously recalibrate local priors. Verified repairs from every participating branch become shared learning
evidence rather than remaining isolated service tickets.

  Completed jobs -> verified diagnosis -> repair type -> property age/materials -> geography -> branch/Van ->
  outcome -> aggregate regional pattern -> confidence threshold -> neighborhood/property prior update -> future
  field verification -> settlement -> recalibration
Evidence can be weighted by sample size, geographic concentration, construction similarity, material similarity, repeated
technician confirmation, outcome confirmation, recency and cross-branch agreement. Patterns are weakened when samples are
small, diagnoses conflict, cohorts are dissimilar, evidence becomes stale or the pattern stops recurring.


11. Geographic Learning Hierarchy
  Company-wide pattern -> Region -> Branch territory -> ZIP -> Neighborhood/Subdivision -> Comparable
  property cohort -> Individual property

More specific evidence can outweigh broader priors when it is sufficiently strong. A client file should expose the distinction
between regional support, neighborhood support and property-specific verification.


12. Pattern Promotion Ladder
  OBSERVED -> CANDIDATE PATTERN -> REGIONALLY SUPPORTED -> VERIFIED PATTERN -> ACTIVE PRIOR ->
  MONITORED FOR DRIFT -> RETIRED / DOWN-WEIGHTED

This prevents isolated technician observations from becoming company truth while allowing genuine field discoveries to
propagate across the organization.

13. Downstream Trades-Runtime Integration
    Subsystem                          Use of property/neighborhood/regional intelligence

    Field Advisor                      Surfaces locally elevated failure modes as inspection/diagnostic hypotheses.

    Call-Fit / Dispatch                Weights proven competence with emerging/local issue classes while preserving governing dispatch constraints.

    Truck Stock Intelligence           Adjusts Van stock toward recurring local repair patterns.

    Warehouse / Procurement            Forecasts and pre-positions parts associated with emerging regional failures.

    Training & Workforce Development Identifies emerging failure classes requiring technician education.

    Pricebook / Profitability          Recalibrates runtime, labor and cost assumptions for recurring regional work.

    Weather Demand Intelligence        Tests whether repair clusters respond to freeze, heat, flooding, groundwater or other environmental events.

    Property Value Intelligence        Models legitimate infrastructure/condition burdens and the value-preservation effect of completed work.

    Universal Analytics                Allows heat maps, trend analysis, cohort comparison, probability reports and regional repair-pattern reporting.


14. Recurring Refresh & Settlement
Event-driven: company work, inspection, permit, listing/sale or ownership change. Weekly: neighborhood
market/listing/comparable movement where practical. Monthly: property valuation and neighborhood momentum. Quarterly:
broader model calibration and nominal-vs-real analysis. On demand: fresh property packet for authorized operational workflows.

Actual future listings, sales, completed repairs and verified field findings become settlement evidence for both valuation and
failure-pattern models.


15. Evidence & Governance Rules
External real-estate/listing/property data is evidence, not automatic truth. Every material property fact should retain source,
observed/retrieved time, freshness, confidence, verification status and conflicts. Approved/licensed APIs and legitimate
public/authorized sources should be preferred over making the architecture dependent on unauthorized scraping.

  Public-property, market, neighborhood and regional-pattern data may inform priors, preparation, routing,
  valuation and diagnostic hypotheses, but must not be represented as verified property fact until corroborated
  by authoritative evidence or field observation. Property-value effects are estimates, not guarantees.

16. Governing Property Intelligence Principle
Trades models the home as an evolving physical and financial asset whose technical condition, repair history,
neighborhood context, regional failure patterns and market exposure change over time. Every verified repair
improves the individual property record and, when appropriately aggregated, improves the company's regional
operating intelligence.
