 Core Principle
 Trades-Runtime does not begin by replacing the existing business stack. It first shadows it, records what actually
 happened, records what Trades would have done, records every human override, then proves or disproves its
 decisions against operational outcomes.


Target operating scale: 13-15 branches | 750+ employees | Midwest region | multi-trade
field service
Primary integration posture: tether to ServiceTitan + ProBooks/other authorized systems first; operate as a read-only or
recommendation layer until evidence supports greater autonomy.

This document captures the architecture, ideas, rules, controls and operating logic established for Trades-Runtime.

1. Executive Summary
Trades-Runtime is a company operating intelligence layer designed to coordinate booking, dispatch, technician
selection, field execution, warranty logic, pricing, inventory, sales turnovers, service quality, management
escalation and continuous operational learning across a large multi-branch trades business.

Its first deployment mode is shadow operation. Existing systems and humans continue running the company.
Trades-Runtime observes the same inputs, produces its own decisions, follows the day as reality changes, and
compares its counterfactual choices with actual outcomes. The goal is not to claim that the AI would have
performed better; the goal is to build call-level and day-level evidence showing when it did, when it did not, and
why.

    Human Authority Rule
    Authorized human decisions always win operationally. Trades-Runtime may recommend against an override and
    explain why, but it never supersedes authorized human recalibration. The disagreement remains preserved as
    evidence.



1.1 Primary objectives
     Answer and book customer calls with shared visibility into membership, equipment, warranty and service
      history.
     Choose the correct technician/Van for the correct call using technical fit, quality, client fit, geography,
      inventory, runtime, profit, margin and development opportunity.
     Minimize unnecessary travel and fuel through geographic/ZIP consolidation without allowing proximity to
      become the final deciding factor.
     Continuously learn technician strengths, weaknesses, role suitability and uncertainty without poisoning the
      model through self-reinforcing assignment bias.
     Use before/during/after photos and video to normalize skill, difficulty, time, workmanship and legitimate
      added cost.
     Track repairs, turnovers, sales, warranties, callbacks, inventory and downstream revenue as connected
      events.
     Provide receipts for recommendations, disagreements, overrides, actual outcomes and counterfactual
      outcomes.
     Allow a field-side AI advisor to be turned on or off per technician/per call.
     Create a canonical live operating state shared across IT, office, dispatch, managers, field technicians and
      client booking interfaces.

1.2 What Trades-Runtime is not
     Not a simple dispatch optimizer.
     Not a leaderboard that equates revenue with technician quality.
     Not a fixed morning route planner.
     Not an autonomous black box that can silently overrule humans.
     Not an AI that treats a photo inference as proof of fault or misconduct.
     Not a fuel-minimization system that sacrifices technical fit or customer outcome.
     Not a system that assumes a 30-day return visit is automatically a technician callback.

2. System Operating Model
2.1 Canonical live state
Trades-Runtime maintains one canonical live company state. Each role receives a role-specific view of that same
state. A completed field event can therefore update technician availability, route position, inventory, booking
capacity, management forecasts, warranty context and dispatch recommendations without each department
maintaining conflicting versions of reality.

Canonical state inputs include customer, equipment, membership, service history, booking state, technician/Van
state, location, inventory, active call state, media evidence, quote state, warranty state, route state, manager
queue, pricebook state and integration health.

 Planned state, actual state, counterfactual state and human override state remain separately queryable. A rebase
 changes the next decision; it does not erase the prior decision.



2.3 Shadow counterfactual
For each decision point, the system asks: "Given only the information available at that moment, what would
Trades-Runtime have done?" This prevents hindsight leakage. Once reality diverges from the recommendation,
the shadow model rebases from the new actual state rather than pretending the original imaginary schedule still
exists.

The resulting evidence model is:
ACTUAL: customer call -> existing booking/dispatch -> actual Van -> actual diagnosis/quote/repair/turnover -> actual
revenue/margin/time/parts.

TRADES: same known inputs -> Trades booking/dispatch -> predicted Van/route -> expected diagnosis/repair/turnover ->
expected revenue/margin/time/parts.

OVERRIDE: Trades recommendation -> authorized human change -> reason -> replacement action -> resulting state ->
observed outcome.




3. Booking & Schedule Precedence
3.1 Booking precedence rule

Already-booked calls establish precedence. The default policy is to preserve customer commitments rather than
optimize the schedule by repeatedly moving customers for marginal route gains.

    Schedule rule
    Preserve existing customer commitments unless the expected operational or customer consequence of preserving
    them exceeds the cost of displacement.



3.2 Demand-based displacement
Demand service can justify rescheduling when the service need materially outweighs the protected
appointment. Displacement is never silent. Trades-Runtime evaluates alternatives, documents why a move is
recommended, estimates customer impact, and requires the appropriate operational workflow for customer
contact/approval.

     Urgency and safety impact.
     Membership/service-level commitments.
     Existing appointment precedence and promised window.
     Available technician competence and inventory.
     Travel and current route position.
     Expected call runtime and downstream schedule damage.
     Likelihood of successful rebooking or cancellation.
     Revenue/contribution consequences, but never revenue alone.
     Customer history, prior disruptions and management sensitivity.



4. Dispatch Intelligence
4.1 Call-Fit Score
Every eligible Van receives a dynamic multi-objective Call-Fit Score. It is not a single sales metric and it is not
distance-first.
Call-Fit = quality + service competence + technical fit + client-tech fit + geolocation + truck inventory + estimated runtime +
expected fixed-dollar contribution + expected margin + schedule impact + exploration opportunity.


4.2 Geographic and ZIP intelligence
The system learns geographic clusters, ZIP patterns, branch boundaries, traffic/travel behavior and common
service corridors. Vans can be preferentially positioned or routed to consolidate travel and reduce fuel cost, but
geographic efficiency is never the final deciding factor. A farther technician can win when expected technical
resolution, inventory coverage, quality or contribution materially outweighs the route savings.

4.3 Dynamic route drift
An 8-12 hour field day will not remain on the route imagined at the start of the day. Trades-Runtime treats drift
as normal operating evidence. After every meaningful event, the remaining schedule is recalculated from the
actual present state.
Morning plan -> event -> actual position/state -> rebase -> next event -> rebase -> close.

Key day-level measures are morning forecast quality, adaptation quality and final-day operating outcome.

4.4 Runtime modeling
Trades-Runtime learns standard call duration by issue, trade, equipment type, access/difficulty profile and
technician. Generic time blocks are progressively replaced by distributions learned from actual work. Runtime
influences not only the current assignment, but the expected feasibility of the next calls in the day.

4.5 Exploration / curveball mechanism
Pure exploitation creates a poisoned model: technicians who historically receive a job type gain more evidence
and opportunity while others appear weak because they are never tested. Trades-Runtime therefore maintains
an explicit exploration mechanism.

      Exploration is controlled and competence-gated, not random assignment.
      The system tracks observed capability separately from uncertainty/lack of opportunity.
      Appropriate technicians can receive selected new opportunities when adjacent skills suggest they can safely
       perform them.
      Exploration results update the technician skill model and reduce uncertainty.
      Long-term workforce development can justify a small short-term optimization tradeoff when risk remains
       acceptable.

6. Visual & Video QA Engine
6.1 Media as field ground truth
Before, during and after photos and video are primary calibration evidence. Media is combined with notes,
equipment information, parts, elapsed time, diagnostic readings, quotes, callbacks and final system
performance. Media inference alone is never treated as definitive proof of fault, misconduct or workmanship
failure.

6.2 What the engine estimates
      Initial equipment condition and equipment age indicators.
      Access difficulty, corrosion, crowding, contamination, wiring complexity, installation condition and other
       effort drivers.
      Likely repair difficulty and expected labor time.
      Workmanship, completeness and visible quality after repair.
      Expected parts/BOM and whether the installed/used parts are consistent with the scope.
      Legitimate added-cost drivers tied to unusual access, condition, procurement or complexity.
      Effort required versus effort demonstrated.
      Potential concerns visible in media that are absent from technician notes, flagged as review items rather
       than accusations.
      Callback risk and documentation sufficiency.

6.3 Skill normalization
Visual/video evidence helps distinguish a slow technician from a difficult repair and distinguishes high revenue
from high technical competence. A technician who completes a severe-access repair correctly in 150 minutes can
outperform a technician who completes an easy repair in 60 minutes if the expected normalized time and
quality support that conclusion.

8. Manager Intervention Queue
Trades-Runtime continuously estimates where management involvement is likely to improve outcome before a
field situation becomes an escalation.

      Repeat visits or unresolved history.
      Angry/escalating customer language.
      Disputed warranty or membership status.
      High-dollar or unusually complex repair decisions.
      Prior declined work now implicated in a failure.
      Technician uncertainty or repeated Field Advisor use.
      Media/notes conflict or documentation gaps.
      Unusually high quote variance.
      Potential callback ambiguity.
      Schedule displacement with material customer impact.
The manager queue is a recommendation and prioritization surface. It does not remove field/office judgment.

9. Field Advisor
9.1 Per-tech/per-call on/off control
The field AI advisor can be enabled or disabled by authorized policy or by the technician/call workflow. When
disabled, Trades-Runtime can continue passive observation where permitted. When enabled, it becomes an
active diagnostic and repair copilot.

9.2 Field context available to the advisor
   Customer and equipment history.
   Prior repair/quote/decline history.
   Parts/labor warranty and membership status.
   Known model/serial information.
   Current truck inventory.
   Pricebook matches.
   Photos/video/readings/notes supplied during the call.
   Likely failure hierarchy and next diagnostic checks.
   Predicted parts, runtime, difficulty and procurement needs.
   Suggested repair/quote options and customer explanation.

9.3 Learning from advice usage
The system can measure whether AI guidance was requested, followed or rejected and what outcome followed.
This is not a simplistic surveillance score. The purpose is to learn where assistance improves outcomes, where
technicians outperform the model, and where the model needs recalibration.



10. Pricebook & Profitability Engine
10.1 ServiceTitan pricebook adaptation
Trades-Runtime ingests the authorized pricebook and compares current pricing with observed repair volume,
parts cost, labor burden, difficulty, access, procurement friction, callback risk, demand, market constraints and
target gross contribution.

Price recommendations should consider both fixed-dollar contribution and percentage margin. High-volume
work can have different optimal economics than rare, high-complexity work.

10.2 Quote-from-field function
One action should be able to combine technician notes, photos/video, equipment context, predicted parts,
difficulty, access, procurement, inventory, warranty and the pricebook into a proposed quote package with
confidence and assumptions.

   Probable repair/scope.
   Expected parts/BOM.
   Part availability and procurement path.
   Expected labor/runtime.
   Difficulty/access modifiers.
   Warranty effect.
   Pricebook item mapping.

11. Truck Inventory Intelligence
Truck inventory is dynamic and technician-specific. Trades-Runtime compares what it predicted would be
required with what ServiceTitan/other systems show was actually consumed, then adjusts recommended stock
by technician skill profile, territory, seasonal call mix and likely future work.

   Inventory fit affects dispatch because it changes first-trip completion probability.
   Actual consumption reconciles the prediction model.
   Frequent emergency procurement becomes evidence of a stocking gap.
   Stock recommendations should consider carrying cost and vehicle capacity, not simply maximize inventory.
   Parts prediction performance becomes a measurable model-quality metric.



12. Revenue, Repair & Sales Attribution
Trades-Runtime keeps the originating service call connected to all downstream outcomes rather than separating
service and sales into unrelated systems.

   Functional repair revenue.
   IAQ revenue.
   Enhancements/accessories.
   Warranty repairs.
   Memberships.
   Equipment turnovers generated.
   Turnovers sold by Comfort Advisors/sales staff.
   Gross contribution and margin.
   Callback or rework cost.
   Attributable downstream revenue from the original service interaction.
This supports fairer analysis of who identifies opportunities, who closes them, and what combination of
technician/customer/call/manager/route conditions produces the strongest company outcome.

16. Shadow-Mode Proof Framework
16.1 Deployment ladder
1. Observe - ingest and normalize data without influencing decisions.
2. Shadow - produce sealed counterfactual recommendations while humans/existing software operate normally.
3. Recommend - surface decisions to staff with no autonomous execution.
4. Human Approves - one-click acceptance/override workflow.
5. Limited Autonomy - narrowly scoped actions with hard constraints and rollback.
6. Autonomous Operations - only after sustained evidence, governance and operational approval.

16.2 Proof metrics
Trades-Runtime should not claim success using revenue alone. Shadow proof should include:

      Gross contribution delta and margin delta.
      First-trip completion.
      Callback/rework rate.
      Diagnostic accuracy.
      Turnovers created and sold.
      Travel/fuel/time efficiency.
      Customer wait and displacement impact.
      Runtime forecast error.
      Inventory prediction accuracy.
      Warranty/callback classification accuracy.
      Quality/workmanship indicators.
      Manager intervention prediction quality.
      Human override win/loss/neutral rate.
      Exploration results and newly discovered technician capability.

18. Decision Rules to Freeze in v0.1
1. Human-authorized recalibration always controls the live operation.
2. No recommendation may erase the recommendation or state that preceded it.
3. Route drift is expected and causes rebasing, not automatic failure scoring.
4. Existing bookings have precedence; displacement must be justified and auditable.
5. Geography/fuel matters but is never the final factor.
6. Technician revenue is not equivalent to technician skill.
7. Skill must be normalized for call difficulty, access, runtime, opportunity mix and customer context.
8. Inside-30-day return visits are reviewed, not automatically charged as callbacks.
9. Previously quoted and declined work must be separated from technician fault.
10. Media can support a concern but cannot alone prove misconduct or diagnostic failure.
11. Unknown warranty status is never silently converted to not-covered.
12. Exploration opportunities are necessary to prevent historical dispatch patterns from poisoning capability
estimates.
13. Output and performance remain separate metrics.
14. Trades-Runtime must be able to explain material decisions with receipts.
15. Actual real-world state always becomes the starting point for the next decision.

21. v0.1 Build Boundary
The first build should favor proof over autonomy. The most valuable v0.1 is a reliable shadow operating system
that can reconstruct calls, technicians/Vans, routes, booking decisions, warranty/membership context, outcomes
and overrides with defensible receipts.

21.1 Minimum viable shadow system
1. Ingest ServiceTitan + ProBooks/authorized operational data.
2. Normalize customers, equipment, calls, Vans/technicians, bookings, jobs, quotes, parts, turnovers and sales.
3. Create canonical event timestamps and call IDs across integrations.
4. Map technician identities to Van identifiers for primary analytics.
5. Implement Full-Day Trajectory, Call-to-Call Ledger and Override/Recalibration Chain.
6. Implement baseline Call-Fit scoring and route rebasing.
7. Implement technician role tags and multi-window dashboards (1 week / 30 / 60 / 90 days / 6 months).
8. Implement 30-day callback/warranty decision support.
9. Implement warranty lookup adapters where authorized.
10. Implement media ingestion and initial before/after visual QA scoring.
11. Implement receipts that make every shadow recommendation reconstructable.
12. Compare actual versus shadow outcomes without allowing future information into the original
recommendation.

21.2 Subsequent capabilities
Once the shadow data is trustworthy, add live Field Advisor, video reasoning, manager queue, quote generation,
pricebook optimization, truck inventory prediction, customer-facing AI call handling and progressively
controlled execution.

22. Success Condition
Trades-Runtime succeeds when management can open any day, any call or any meaningful system
disagreement and see: what the company knew at that moment, what existing operations did, what Trades-
Runtime recommended, whether a human changed it, why, how the remaining day adapted, what physically
occurred in the field, what the customer received, and what measurable result followed.

 North Star

23. Communications, Recognition and Live Goals
Trades-Runtime includes an internal Teams-style communications layer built on the same canonical event
stream as booking, dispatch, field operations, warehouse, management and I.T. Managers can create, manage,
archive and moderate company, branch, department, trade, dispatch, sales, field, warehouse, project and
temporary incident chats or channels according to permissions.

23.1 Automated recognition
Trades-Runtime may automatically celebrate operational achievements after quality gates are satisfied.
Recognition is context-aware and must not reward raw revenue alone.

   High repair/service tickets completed successfully.
   Replacement turnovers generated and equipment replacements closed, preserving attribution to both the
    originating Van and the closing Comfort Advisor.
   Difficult jobs completed correctly, especially when difficulty-normalized duration and quality are
    exceptional.
   High-value repairs with successful completion and acceptable callback/quality outcomes.
   Exceptional duration-adjusted performance, first-trip completion, diagnostic quality and sustained
    improvement.

23.2 Daily mission board
Role-appropriate dashboards display configurable daily goals for company, region, branch, team and role. Core
measures include repair/service sales, equipment sales, calls completed, turnovers generated, turnovers closed,
first-trip completion and other approved KPIs. Each goal displays target, actual, expected pace at the current
time, remaining gap and projected end-of-day finish.



24. Dynamic Pricebook, Truck Stock and Supply Intelligence
The ServiceTitan pricebook is the initial shadow baseline. Trades-Runtime observes actual economics before
recommending changes. Pricebook, truck-stock and procurement recommendations remain subordinate to
authorized human control.

24.1 Pricebook recommendation inputs
   Repair frequency and current/predicted demand.
   Whether acquisition is required or the item already exists on the assigned Van.
   Current, last and average part cost.
   Expected contribution and approved premium profit-margin targets.
   Availability and lead time.
   Shipping/freight cost and delay when required.
   Warehouse or distributor procurement burden.
   Labor, job difficulty, access difficulty and expected runtime.
   Exact OEM part number, approved substitute, vendor/SKU and compatibility evidence.
   Historical returns/failures, first-trip completion effect and actual realized margin.

Inventory location is operationally material. Trades-Runtime distinguishes ON VAN, NEARBY VAN, BRANCH
STOCK, CENTRAL WAREHOUSE, LOCAL DISTRIBUTOR, SHIPPED and BACKORDERED states when estimating job
economics and completion probability.

24.2 Manager override and lock
Authorized managers can ACCEPT, OVERRIDE or LOCK any pricebook, part-selection or stock recommendation.
A lock prevents automatic recalibration until an authorized human changes or releases it. Trades-Runtime may
continue collecting evidence and recommend reconsideration, but it may not supersede the lock. Every action is
preserved in Chain 3 with who, when, why, prior recommendation and subsequent outcome.

24.3 Van-specific stock optimization
Trades-Runtime learns an optimal inventory profile for each Van using its territory, call mix, technician skill
profile, actual consumption, first-trip completion, predicted demand, procurement lead time, carrying cost and
exploration needs. It recommends additions, increases, reductions and items that should be eliminated or held at
minimal quantities.

24.4 Warehouse chat and fulfillment queue
Warehouse receives a dedicated operational chat/queue generated from live job demand and truck-stock
consumption. Requests identify call/job, Van, required part and exact part number, quantity, stock locations,
urgency, source/availability, shipping or procurement requirement, and whether replenishment is required
after fulfillment.

Suggested workflow: REQUESTED -> CLAIMED -> PICKING -> READY -> TRANSFER -> DELIVERED -> INSTALLED
-> RECONCILED. Purchasing authority remains human unless explicitly delegated later.



25. Weather and Demand Intelligence
Each branch/service territory maintains three synchronized weather windows: the past 7 days, the current
week/current conditions, and the forward 7-day forecast. Trades-Runtime learns branch-specific relationships
between environmental conditions and HVAC, plumbing and electrical demand rather than relying on a single
generic weather rule.

25.1 Environmental features
   Temperature, heat intensity/duration and heat index.
   Cold intensity/duration, snow/ice and freeze-thaw behavior.
   Humidity and dew point for HVAC demand and latent-load/comfort effects.
   Precipitation, heavy rainfall, flash-flood conditions and storm activity.
   Wind and severe-weather disruption.
   Rapid temperature transitions and multi-day weather lag.
   Extreme or black-swan operating states such as blizzards, dangerous heat events, flash floods and
    exceptional rainfall.

25.2 Demand outputs
Weather features are correlated with branch history including bookings, call types, equipment ages,
memberships, callbacks, runtimes, sales, turnovers, parts consumption and staffing. Outputs include predicted
call volume by trade/call type, uncertainty range, peak windows, staffing/capacity risk, Van-positioning
implications, truck-stock recommendations, warehouse procurement signals, manager-coverage needs and goal
adjustments.

Extreme-event mode increases uncertainty bands and predicts event-specific call mix instead of merely
multiplying normal demand.

25.3 Lunar/Earth-tide plumbing hypothesis
Lunar-cycle and Earth-tide variables may be retained as experimental plumbing-demand features because
lunar/solar gravitational forcing can produce measurable groundwater and aquifer-pressure changes in some
environments. Trades-Runtime must not assume that lunar phase itself causes residential plumbing leaks. The
feature must earn predictive weight from branch-specific historical data after controlling for weather,
precipitation, soil/groundwater conditions, seasonality and other confounders. If it provides no incremental
predictive lift, its model weight should approach zero.

25.4 Forecast calibration
Every branch forecast is reconciled against actual demand. Forecast error, missed call mix and operational
consequences are preserved so each territory's weather-response model improves over multiple seasons.



26. Maintenance Scheduling and Equipment-Age Opportunity
Routing
Trades-Runtime uses company installation records, equipment age, warranty, membership, maintenance
history, prior repairs/quotes and current demand to schedule and route maintenance. The objective is to put a
qualified available technician on the correct job without delaying legitimate demand merely to preserve a sales
opportunity.

26.1 Technician operational tags
   Maintenance Tech Level 1, Level 2 and Level 3.
   Service Tech Level 1, Level 2 and Level 3.
   Selling Tech.
   Repair Tech.
   Warranty.
   Comfort Advisor.
   Manager.

Tags are internally editable. Authorized human recalibration always wins; Trades-Runtime may recommend
against a change and explain why, but cannot supersede it. All changes remain in Chain 3.

26.2 Equipment-age scheduling
Maintenance capacity should intentionally account for the age distribution of company-installed equipment.
Newer systems, middle-aged systems and older systems are distributed through the appropriate
maintenance/service ladder rather than being assigned without context.

Systems around 10 years old and older receive progressively greater weighting toward qualified Selling Techs
because replacement probability is generally more relevant as systems age. The older/problematic the system,
the stronger the weighting toward technicians proven capable of both correct technical service and appropriate
replacement-opportunity handling.

26.3 Demand-first dispatch rule
Sales opportunity is a technician-matching factor, not permission to delay qualified service. If an appropriate
qualified technician is available, Trades-Runtime should dispatch rather than hold the customer waiting for an

27. Updated Governing Rules
   Reality always becomes the next starting state; route drift across an 8-12 hour day is expected evidence, not
    an automatic failure.
   Preserve Planned State, Actual State and Counterfactual State, plus the human Override/Recalibration Chain.
   Already-booked customers have precedence; avoid rescheduling unless demand/urgency justifies
    displacement and the decision is auditable.
   Humans retain authority everywhere. Trades-Runtime can warn, recommend and explain, but authorized
    human override or lock controls the live operation.
   Optimize quality, competence, correct technician fit, client fit, geography, inventory, runtime, expected fixed
    profit and expected margin together; fuel/geography never become the sole deciding factor.
   Use controlled exploration so historical assignment patterns do not poison technician skill models.
   Use photos and video with notes, parts, timing and outcomes to normalize skill, difficulty, effort and
    legitimate added cost; media inference alone is not proof of misconduct.
   Use one canonical live operating state with heartbeat/event synchronization across I.T., office, dispatch,
    managers, field technicians, warehouse and client booking.
   Standard technician performance windows are 1 week, 30 days, 60 days, 90 days and 6 months.
   Weather, demand, inventory, pricebook and maintenance scheduling continuously feed one another, while
    all shadow recommendations remain reconstructable from contemporaneous evidence.
