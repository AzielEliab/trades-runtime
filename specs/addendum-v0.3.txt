                                               Trades-Runtime
     Critical Architecture Addendum v0.3 - Failure Containment, Economics, Digital Twin & Operating Controls

Scope. This addendum freezes the critical operating-system concerns that sit around the previously defined dispatch,
maintenance, weather, inventory, pricebook, field-advisor, coherence, evidence and human-override architecture.


Economic rule added
   Labor x cost x realized profit must be first-class metrics. Trades-Runtime must not optimize ticket
   revenue, margin percentage, or technician speed in isolation. Every job should ultimately reconcile the labor
   consumed and its fully loaded cost against parts, acquisition/procurement cost, warranty recovery,
   callback/rework cost and collected revenue.


1. Job Economics & Labor Profitability Engine
Each completed call should produce a predicted and realized economic record. Technician quality remains separate from
economics, but economics must be visible to dispatch, pricebook, staffing, training and company simulation.

   Metric                        Definition / use

   Labor hours                   Actual technician time attributable to the job, with travel/onsite/return labor separated where useful.

   Loaded labor cost             Hourly wage or approved labor-cost basis plus employer burden/overhead allocation according to company policy.

   Parts cost                    Actual consumed parts at real acquisition cost, not only pricebook retail value.

   Procurement cost              Freight, supply-house trip, transfer, rush shipping and other acquisition burden.

   Revenue                       Invoiced and, where available, collected revenue attributable to the job.

   Warranty recovery             Expected and actual manufacturer/distributor/labor reimbursement.

   Callback/rework cost          Labor, parts, travel and customer concessions created by a return or workmanship issue.

   Gross contribution            Revenue and recoveries less direct labor, parts, procurement and directly attributable rework costs.

   Contribution per labor hour   Gross contribution divided by productive labor hours; never used alone as a skill score.

   Margin %                      Gross contribution relative to revenue, alongside fixed-dollar contribution.

   Opportunity cost              Effect of consuming scarce technician time that could have served higher-priority demand.


Core reconciliation: Collected/recognized revenue + warranty recovery - parts - procurement - loaded labor -
callback/rework - directly attributable concessions = realized contribution. Predicted economics should be scored against
realized economics so pricing and dispatch learn from actual profitability.

Do not turn this into a technician leaderboard that rewards rushing, overselling, cherry-picking or avoiding difficult work.
Difficulty, access, warranty, opportunity, customer mix, job type and quality must remain normalization variables.


2. Failure Containment & Degraded Modes
Trades-Runtime must assume dependencies fail. Define behavior for ServiceTitan outage, manufacturer API failure, stale
GPS, missing weather, warehouse-count conflict, event-bus delay, AI-model outage and connector timeout. Every subsystem
needs a known degraded mode: continue, freeze, fall back, queue, or require human action. Trades must never become a
single point of company failure.


3. Master Data & Source-of-Truth Rules
Freeze the authoritative owner for customer, property, equipment, serial/model, membership, appointment, Van/technician,
pricebook, inventory, warranty, invoice, payment and accounting records. Trades may maintain the canonical live operating
state without silently replacing the legal/system-of-record owner. Conflicts must enter a reconciliation queue rather than being
guessed away.


4. Identity Resolution Graph
Maintain persistent Customer -> Property -> System -> Component -> Job -> Part relationships. Resolve duplicate customers,
moved equipment, previous homeowners, installation records, serial/model variants, warranty registrations and historical calls
so intelligence accumulates around the same real-world asset instead of fragmenting across records.


5. Event Idempotency & Reconciliation
Every heartbeat/event requires a unique event ID, source, sequence/timestamp and processing state. Duplicate events must
not create duplicate bookings, revenue, inventory depletion or recognition. Periodic reconciliation against source systems
must detect missing, delayed, duplicated and conflicting events.


6. Model, Policy & Configuration Versioning
Every recommendation must preserve the model version, ruleset, pricebook version, inventory snapshot, technician/Van
profile, weather/demand forecast, evidence state and policy configuration that produced it. A later audit must be able to
reconstruct the decision exactly without hindsight leakage.


7. Rollback & Domain Kill Switches
Provide company-, region-, branch- and subsystem-level rollback to a known-good version. Separate controls should exist for
AI Booking, Dispatch, Pricebook, Inventory, Warehouse, Warranty, Field Advisor, Communications and other autonomous
domains. A broken warranty connector must not require disabling dispatch.


8. Data Freshness & Expiration
Attach freshness to operational facts. Inventory observed weeks ago, stale GPS, old warranty responses and outdated
customer/equipment records should lose decision weight. Critical decisions should FLAG/HOLD when required evidence is
too stale. Freshness thresholds can vary by data type.


9. Exception Queues & Ownership
Warnings must become owned work. Create queues for unresolved warranty, inventory conflicts, duplicate identity, possible
callback, price anomaly, manager intervention, media conflict, stale evidence, failed connector, reconciliation mismatch and
other exceptions. Each item needs owner, severity, age, escalation path and resolution receipt.


10. Customer Communication Orchestration
Coordinate confirmations, ETA, technician-en-route notices, delays, displacement/rescheduling, parts arrival, maintenance
reminders, quote follow-up and turnover scheduling. AI communication must not invent arrival times, warranties, pricing,
inventory, approvals or commitments. Material customer promises require authoritative data or human approval according to
policy.


11. No-Show, Cancellation & Confirmation Modeling
Appointment reliability affects the trajectory. Model confirmation state and cancellation/no-show probability as scheduling
inputs, while avoiding unfair or opaque customer treatment. The model should improve capacity planning rather than deny
service based on speculative behavior.
12. Protected Capacity & Demand Reservation
Weather intelligence should be able to reserve capacity before demand arrives. Filling every future slot with low-urgency
maintenance may be locally efficient but harmful when a heat wave, freeze, flood or other surge is forecast. Trades should
recommend protected service capacity with an explicit cost of holding it.


13. Cross-Branch Resource Borrowing
Treat the branch network as one constrained system. Trades can recommend lending Vans, qualified technicians, parts,
warehouse stock, Comfort Advisors, managers or booking capacity between branches while calculating the donor-branch cost
and receiving-branch benefit.


14. Skill Decay, Certification & Recency
Capability is not permanent. Track licenses/certifications, manufacturer training, equipment families, time since last
demonstrated repair, recent outcomes and opportunity exposure. Separate lack of recent opportunity from evidence of
declining competence.


15. Training & Workforce Development Engine
Convert repeated diagnostic gaps, runtime variance, callback patterns and Field Advisor dependence into training
recommendations. Training should create a measurable before/after loop so Trades learns whether the intervention actually
improved performance.


16. Parts Supersession & Compatibility Graph
Build Equipment <-> Component <-> OEM Part <-> Supersession <-> Approved Substitute relationships. Record harnesses,
adapters, compatibility constraints, vendor SKUs, return/failure history and evidence source. This prevents text-search
similarity from becoming false compatibility.


17. Accounting & Realized-Profit Reconciliation
Close the loop from quote and invoice to payment, parts cost, labor cost, warranty reimbursement, customer concession,
callback/rework and final realized contribution. The pricebook and dispatch engines should learn from realized economics, not
only booked or invoiced revenue.


18. Causal Evaluation
Trades must distinguish correlation from improvement caused by its decisions. Use shadow comparisons, controlled rollouts,
matched cohorts and other defensible evaluation methods where feasible. A good outcome after a Trades recommendation
does not by itself prove the recommendation caused the outcome.


19. Metric-Gaming / Goodhart Protection
Assume behavior will adapt to measured KPIs. Balance revenue, contribution, quality, callbacks, diagnostic accuracy,
customer impact, runtime, first-trip completion and difficulty. Monitor for systematic gaming such as unnecessary turnovers,
rushed closes, cherry-picking, inflated tickets or avoidance of difficult calls.


20. Employee Challenge & Correction Mechanism
Technicians and authorized staff should be able to challenge a callback classification, skill inference, media interpretation or
other material assessment and attach evidence. The original assessment is preserved; the challenge, reviewer decision and
downstream model correction become receipts.
21. Privacy, Consent & Retention Boundaries
Define what photos, video, call recordings, customer data, equipment data, technician-performance data and AI inferences
may be collected, analyzed, retained and shown by role. Retention/deletion rules and access boundaries should be explicit
before broad deployment.


22. Disaster Recovery & Business Continuity
Define backup, restore testing, recovery objectives, credential recovery, secondary storage and an operating procedure for a
full Trades outage. The company must be able to run manually or through existing systems while Trades is unavailable.
23. Trades Operational Constitution
These are non-optimizable rules. Learning systems may adjust weights and recommendations, but may not rewrite these
principles without authorized governance.

• Authorized human control supersedes AI within the person's granted authority.

• Safety and legal/compliance constraints beat revenue optimization.

• Customer demand and timely qualified service beat preservation of a sales opportunity.

• Verified evidence beats model confidence.

• Unknown does not equal false, not-covered, unavailable, or failed.

• Never invent inventory, warranty, price, customer, equipment, schedule or technician facts.

• Never silently displace an existing customer.

• Never materially penalize a technician from unverified inference alone.

• Every consequential autonomous action must be reconstructable from point-in-time evidence.

• Every autonomous subsystem requires a human-accessible fallback or degraded mode.

• Learning may change model weights; it may not silently change constitutional rules.

• Revenue, margin, labor efficiency and profit must remain quality- and context-adjusted rather than becoming standalone
employee-worth scores.


24. Company Digital Twin / Simulation Layer
Once shadow trajectories, staffing, weather, equipment age, demand, inventory, pricebook, labor economics and branch
resources share one canonical state, Trades-Runtime can become a digital twin of the operating company. Management can
simulate changes before deploying them.

Example simulations: add two Service Level 2 Vans to a branch; raise or lower a repair price; reserve maintenance capacity
during a forecast heat wave; move stock between branches; change technician role mix; alter maintenance-age routing; add a
warehouse transfer route; or test a new staffing schedule.

   Simulation objective: predict not only revenue but calls served, customer delay, labor hours, loaded labor
   cost, parts/procurement burden, first-trip completion, quality/callback risk, fixed-dollar contribution, margin,
   capacity utilization and downstream sales/turnover effects.


25. Integrated Economic Objective
Trades-Runtime should optimize a balanced operating objective rather than a single KPI. One conceptual form is:

   Operational Value = Customer Demand Served + Quality + First-Trip Completion + Realized
   Contribution + Workforce Development - Customer Delay - Rework/Callbacks - Travel/Procurement
   Waste - Capacity Risk - Evidence/Uncertainty Penalties

Within Realized Contribution, labor is explicit: revenue/recoveries - parts - procurement - loaded labor -
rework/concessions. This ensures that a $1,500 repair requiring extraordinary labor and multiple return trips is not treated as
economically identical to a $1,500 repair completed correctly on the first trip with stocked parts.
