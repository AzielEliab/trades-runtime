                                            Trades-Runtime
                          Cross-Department Relationship & Turnover Matrix - Addendum v0.5

Purpose. Create a measurable HVAC-Plumbing-Electrical relationship engine that recognizes legitimate cross-trade
opportunities discovered during primary service calls, tracks their complete outcomes and economics, and learns which
Vans/technicians create value across departments without allowing secondary sales opportunity to become the primary
reason for dispatch.


1. Constitutional Routing Rule
  Cross-department opportunity is a secondary routing signal only. It may never manufacture the
  candidate pool or become the primary reason a technician is sent.

Trades first identifies technicians who are qualified and appropriate for the customer's actual requested service. Normal
demand, safety, technical capability, availability, quality, client fit, geography, inventory, runtime and other governing
dispatch factors establish the valid candidate pool. Only then may a legitimate secondary cross-trade opportunity contribute
a bounded tie-breaking or ranking weight.

Example: a customer has a 1-year-old furnace and a 15-year-old water heater. Trades must not send an HVAC Selling Tech
primarily because the old water heater might create a Plumbing opportunity. The correct HVAC technician is chosen for the
HVAC call. Cross-trade context becomes relevant only among otherwise appropriate candidates.


2. Cross-Department Relationship Matrix
   Origin        Receiving          Representative legitimate observations

   HVAC          Plumbing           Leaking/aging water heater, visible piping leak, drain/condensate crossover, gas-piping concern.

   HVAC          Electrical         Panel/service concern, unsafe wiring, circuit capacity, disconnect/feed issue, generator/service opportunity.

   Plumbing      HVAC               Aging HVAC equipment, comfort issue, visible condensate/HVAC condition, obvious equipment concern.

   Plumbing      Electrical         Panel condition, water exposure near electrical equipment, water-heater circuit/service concern.

   Electrical    HVAC               HVAC electrical failure pattern, aging equipment, service-capacity implications for replacement.

   Electrical    Plumbing           Visible water-heater/leak/sump/plumbing condition encountered during legitimate electrical work.



3. Cross-Trade Event Chain
  PRIMARY CALL -> LEGITIMATE OBSERVATION -> EVIDENCE/CONTEXT -> CUSTOMER
  DISCUSSION/CONSENT -> CROSS-DEPARTMENT TURNOVER -> BOOKED/NOT BOOKED ->
  RECEIVING TRADE DISPATCH -> COMPLETED/NOT COMPLETED -> SOLD/NOT SOLD ->
  LABOR/PARTS/COST -> REALIZED CONTRIBUTION -> CALLBACK/QUALITY -> MODEL UPDATE

The originating call, originating Van, receiving department, receiving Van, equipment/condition observed, customer response
and final financial/quality outcome remain linked through receipts.


4. Equipment & Property Opportunity Context
Secondary opportunity scoring should use actual property/equipment evidence rather than crude age-only rules. Examples
include water-heater age, visible leakage/corrosion, prior service history, warranty, panel age/type/capacity/condition, HVAC
age/repair history, known electrical or plumbing observations, and supporting technician media/notes.
Equipment age can increase the probability that a legitimate need exists, but age alone does not establish failure, justify a
turnover, or override the customer's primary service need.


5. Originating Van x Receiving Trade Matrix
    Metric                           Example interpretation

    Legitimate opportunities observed Evidence-supported cross-trade conditions recognized.

    Turnovers offered                Opportunities actually discussed/offered under company policy.

    Bookings accepted                Customers who accepted receiving-department booking.

    Receiving calls completed        Booked turnovers actually run by the other trade.

    Jobs sold                        Receiving-trade work sold after evaluation.

    Booking conversion %             Accepted bookings / qualified turnovers offered.

    Completion %                     Receiving calls completed / accepted bookings.

    Sales conversion %               Sold work / completed receiving calls.

    Revenue attributed               Receiving-department revenue traceable to originating event.

    Realized contribution            Revenue/recovery less labor, parts, procurement, rework/concessions.

    False/unsubstantiated rate       Turnovers later found unsupported or materially overstated.

    Customer impact                  Complaints, cancellations, positive outcomes and avoidable disruption.



6. Secondary Dispatch Weight
Cross-trade capability may help break a close call-fit decision between technicians who are already appropriate for the
primary call. It must not elevate a materially worse primary-call candidate merely because that person historically creates
more cross-department sales.

   Rule: Primary Call Fit -> Qualified Candidate Pool -> Governing Dispatch Score -> bounded
   Cross-Department Relationship Weight -> Final Recommendation.

The system should preserve the base score and the secondary adjustment so managers can see whether cross-trade
opportunity changed the recommendation and by how much.


7. Cross-Department Relationship Strength
Trades should learn relationship strength for HVAC <-> Plumbing, Plumbing <-> Electrical and HVAC <-> Electrical at
company, branch, team and individual Van/technician-era levels. Relationship strength should consider opportunity quality,
booking conversion, receiving-trade validation, sold work, realized contribution, customer impact and false-opportunity rate.

A technician who reliably notices genuine adjacent-trade problems can receive positive cross-department recognition. A
technician who generates many low-quality leads should not receive the same score simply because turnover count is high.


8. Missed Opportunity Calibration
Trades may also detect a possible missed cross-trade opportunity when later evidence indicates that a visible,
long-standing condition was likely present during an earlier call. This is a calibration observation, not automatic employee
fault. Media availability, visibility, scope of the original call, technician role, customer interaction and evidence strength must
be considered before any performance implication.


9. Cross-Department Attribution Economics
  Economic attribution must follow the full chain: Originating Van -> turnover -> receiving department ->
  receiving Van -> sold work -> labor -> parts -> procurement -> warranty/recovery -> rework -> realized
  contribution.

Management should be able to see both departmental and originating contribution without double-counting company
revenue. The originating technician may receive turnover attribution while the receiving technician/Comfort Advisor receives
execution/sales attribution. Company financial reporting counts the underlying dollars once.


10. Time-Window Matrix
Cross-department KPIs should use the same frozen analysis windows as the technician profit matrix: 1 week, 1 month, 3
months, 6 months, 9 months and 12 months, with sample-size/confidence indicators. This allows management to
distinguish a short hot streak from durable cross-trade capability.


11. Receipt Requirements
Every material cross-trade event belongs in the full Trades receipt database. Required lineage includes primary call, origin
department/Van, evidence, equipment context, opportunity score, whether the cross-trade signal affected dispatch, customer
consent/response, turnover creation, receiving booking, receiving Van, final diagnosis, sold/not sold, revenue, labor, parts,
realized contribution, callback/quality outcome, model version and any human override.


12. Company-Level Cross-Trade Dashboard
The company dashboard should answer questions such as: How much Plumbing opportunity originated from HVAC? What
percentage booked? What percentage was validated by plumbers? What sold? What became realized contribution? Which
branches have the strongest HVAC-Plumbing relationship? Which Vans generate high-quality adjacent-trade opportunities?
Where are opportunities being lost between turnover and booking? Where are high turnover counts producing poor
validation or customer outcomes?


13. Frozen Safeguards
   Safeguard                   Requirement

   Primary-service integrity   Never route primarily to manufacture an adjacent-trade opportunity.

   Qualification first         Cross-trade weighting only applies after the valid primary candidate pool exists.

   Demand first                Do not delay qualified service to wait for a better cross-selling technician.

   Evidence                    Age/opportunity indicators do not equal verified failure.

   Customer integrity          No invented urgency, defect, warranty, safety issue or sales claim.

   No raw lead gaming          Reward validated quality and realized outcomes, not turnover volume alone.

   No automatic punishment     Possible missed opportunity is a review/calibration signal, not automatic fault.

   No double counting          Origin attribution and receiving attribution may coexist; company dollars count once.

   Human authority             Authorized managers may override; the original recommendation and outcome remain receipted.


  Architecture result: HVAC, Plumbing and Electrical become an interconnected service graph while
  preserving the integrity of the customer's primary reason for service. Trades learns who opens legitimate
  doors for other departments, how reliably those opportunities convert, and what they actually contribute after
  labor and cost.
