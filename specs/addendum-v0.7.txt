                                             Trades-Runtime
                            Universal Analytics & Human Review Layer - Addendum v0.7

Purpose. Make every authorized Trades-Runtime metric, event, receipt, model output and operational relationship
queryable and reviewable by humans through reports, graphs, scales, scorecards, probability analyses, forecasts,
simulations and performance models.


Core Requirement
  If Trades has the authorized data, a human should be able to interrogate it, compare it, model it,
  visualize it and generate a reviewable report from it.

1. Universal Query Layer
Authorized users should be able to ask natural-language or structured questions across company, region, branch, trade,
department, Van, technician era, customer/property/equipment, job, part, time period, operating mode, model version and
receipt type. Reports should not require engineering work for every new question.

Example: "Show Plumbing -> HVAC turnovers for Branches 4-8 over the last 90 days, conversion %, realized contribution,
receiving labor hours and originating Van, compared with the prior 90 days." Trades resolves the requested dimensions,
applies permissions, produces the analysis and preserves the exact query/report definition.


2. Supported Human-Review Outputs
   Output family              Examples

   Reports                    Operational, branch, department, Van, technician-era, executive, exception and audit reports.

   Graphs                     Line, bar, scatter, distribution, trend, variance and comparison visualizations.

   Heat maps / matrices       Cross-department, Chain D coordination, branch, inventory, skill, turnover and failure relationships.

   Scales                     0-100, 0-1000 or policy-defined normalized scores with visible calculation methodology.

   Scorecards                 KPI actuals, targets, attainment %, trends, sample size, confidence and context adjustment.

   Probability reports        Outcome likelihood, uncertainty range, calibration, evidence strength and alternative scenarios.

   Forecasts                  Demand, call type, staffing, inventory, labor, revenue, contribution, weather response and capacity.

   Models                     Labor x cost x profit, dispatch, trajectory, pricebook, inventory, cross-trade, causal and digital-twin models.

   Performance reports        Actual vs predicted, before vs after, branch vs branch, Van vs cohort, policy/model version comparisons.

   Solution reports           Root causes, ranked interventions, predicted impact, implementation cost and measured results.



3. Four Analytical Levels
   Level                      Question answered

   Descriptive                What happened?

   Diagnostic                 Why did it happen?

   Predictive                 What is likely to happen next, with what probability/uncertainty?

   Prescriptive               What actions should humans consider, what are the tradeoffs, and what is predicted to happen under each?
4. Any-Metric Composition
The analytics layer should support arbitrary combinations of authorized metrics rather than a fixed dashboard catalog.
Examples include labor hours vs realized contribution; technician difficulty vs callback rate; weather severity vs call-type mix;
dispatch distance vs first-trip completion; cross-department turnovers vs equipment age; Chain D failures vs customer delay;
pricebook change vs margin; or Field Advisor use vs diagnostic outcome.

Metrics may be grouped, filtered, ranked, normalized, trended, correlated, segmented and compared across supported time
windows, while preserving sample-size and uncertainty warnings.


5. Probability & Uncertainty Reports
Probability outputs must never be presented as certainty. Reports should distinguish prediction confidence, evidence
strength, source quality, cross-source agreement, verification status, sample size and calibration history.

   Example: 78% predicted probability of a next-day no-cool surge; evidence strength HIGH; weather-source
   agreement HIGH; branch-specific historical sample 41 comparable days; forecast status ESTIMATED, not
   realized.

6. Actual vs Predicted / Performed Reports
Every predictive subsystem should support settlement reports comparing forecast to actual performance. Examples:
predicted vs actual call demand, repair duration, parts usage, dispatch outcome, labor cost, margin, cross-trade conversion,
customer displacement, callback rate and solution impact.

Performance reporting should distinguish model accuracy from operational outcome. A good operational result does not
automatically prove the forecast was accurate, and an accurate forecast does not prove management chose the best
response.
7. Universal Scope & Time Controls
   Dimension                   Examples

   Organization                Company, region, branch, department, trade, team.

   People/operations           Van, technician era, manager role, dispatch group, warehouse group.

   Assets                      Customer, property, equipment, component, part/SKU, inventory location.

   Workflow                    Booking, dispatch, field, turnover, warehouse, warranty, callback, Chain D handoff.

   Mode                        Watcher, Shadow, Recommendation, Human-Approved, Limited Autonomy, Autonomous.

   Time                        Hour/day/week/month/quarter/year; frozen KPI windows 1 week, 1, 3, 6, 9 and 12 months.

   Version                     Model, policy, pricebook, inventory snapshot, ruleset, deployment and branch maturity state.



8. Drill-Down to Evidence & Receipts
Every chart, score, probability, report and model should permit authorized drill-down from aggregate result to the underlying
calls, events, receipts, evidence packets and calculation lineage. Human reviewers should be able to determine where the
number came from rather than treating the visualization as an opaque truth source.

  Aggregate -> cohort -> event/job -> decision receipt -> evidence packet -> source/version.

9. Mandatory Report Metadata
   Field                       Required disclosure

   Scope / filters             Exactly what records, branches, roles, modes and dates were included/excluded.

   Data sources                ServiceTitan/ProBooks/Trades-native/connector/manual or other authorized sources.

   Sample size                 Number of jobs/events/observations supporting the result.

   Formula / methodology       How the metric, scale, savings estimate or probability was calculated.

   Assumptions                 Operational/model assumptions that materially affect interpretation.

   Confidence / uncertainty    Model confidence, evidence strength and relevant interval/range.

   Status                      Observed, estimated, attributed, reconciled/realized, simulated or unresolved.

   Version lineage             Model, ruleset, policy, pricebook and report-definition version where applicable.

   Receipt references          Traceable lineage to underlying decision/evidence records.



10. Labor x Cost x Profit Analytics
The analytics layer must treat labor and realized economics as first-class dimensions. Users should be able to graph or
model revenue, loaded labor cost, parts, procurement, warranty recovery, callbacks/rework, contribution, margin and
contribution per labor hour by any authorized operational dimension.

Profit metrics remain quality- and context-adjusted. Reports must not convert raw profit into an employee-worth score or
reward rushing, cherry-picking or inappropriate selling.


11. Cross-Department & Chain D Analytics
The same engine should report HVAC <-> Plumbing <-> Electrical relationship strength, turnovers, validation, conversion
and realized contribution, while also analyzing Office/Dispatch/Field/Management/Warehouse/Sales/Accounting
coordination health. Users can compare cross-trade opportunity creation with cross-functional communication failure without
mixing the two concepts.


12. Digital Twin & Scenario Reporting
Simulation outputs should be reportable with explicit assumptions and uncertainty. Humans may ask what happens if staffing
changes, a branch borrows Vans, prices move, stock is repositioned, maintenance capacity is protected, weather becomes
extreme, or a Chain D workflow fix is implemented.

  Simulation results must be labeled SIMULATED/ESTIMATED until reconciled against real-world results.

13. Report Generation Modes
   Mode                          Behavior

   On demand                     Human asks a question and Trades generates the analysis immediately from authorized data.

   Saved report                  Human saves a reusable query/report definition for later reruns.

   Scheduled                     Approved reports can run on a defined cadence for management review.

   Event-triggered               A report can be generated when an approved condition occurs, such as a major variance or failure threshold.

   Settlement                    Automatically compare prior prediction/recommendation with realized outcome after the result becomes known.

   Audit                         Reconstruct a historical decision or metric using point-in-time data and version lineage.



14. Human Review & Decision Boundary
Analytics informs humans; it does not convert probability into fact or automatically authorize high-consequence action.
Human reviewers can inspect assumptions, challenge classifications, modify filters, request alternate models, accept/reject
proposed solutions and trace every material result back to evidence.


15. Anti-Misleading Visualization Rules
Trades should flag tiny samples, missing data, stale sources, selection bias, incomparable cohorts, estimated values, wide
uncertainty and model drift. Scales must expose their definition. Rankings must show sample size/context. Estimated
savings must never be displayed as realized savings. Correlation must not be labeled causation without appropriate
evaluation.


16. Architecture Integration
  Canonical State + Chains A/B/C/D + Cross-Department Matrix + Economics + Weather/Demand +
  Inventory/Pricebook + Evidence/Receipts + Digital Twin -> Universal Analytics Layer -> Human
  Review.

The analytics layer therefore becomes the common human interrogation surface across the entire Trades-Runtime
architecture rather than another isolated dashboard subsystem.

18. Frozen Requirement
  Any authorized metric in Trades-Runtime must be capable of human-readable reporting,
  visualization, comparison, modeling and evidence-backed review at the appropriate scope. No
  important model output should exist only as an internal machine value that management cannot
  inspect and challenge.
